The acute food-security crisis escalated before agricultural destruction reached its later scale, suggesting that access restrictions, supply disruption and market collapse were central to the early shock.

04

How They Interact

Gaza’s food crisis cannot be explained by agricultural destruction alone. Severe food insecurity emerged early, while damage to local food-producing capacity continued to accumulate over time.

Food insecurity rose early while agricultural damage kept accumulating

Key finding: Severe food insecurity was already widespread in late 2023, while agricultural damage continued to deepen over the following months. This suggests that access disruption and the progressive loss of local production capacity were overlapping pressures, rather than a single linear cause.

05

Recovery

Recovery is not only about restoring food access. It also requires rebuilding Gaza’s damaged agricultural production capacity and directing limited resources toward the areas with the greatest losses.

Priority Model

How the model works

Priority is based on two transparent signals: recorded greenhouse destruction and whether a governorate is already a current rehabilitation focus.

Assessed site counts are shown separately as a coverage indicator and are not converted into a confidence score.

Governorate Priority score
Interpretation: This ranking is an illustrative decision-support heuristic, not an official or validated allocation model. It gives greater weight to recorded greenhouse destruction and adds a smaller penalty where current rehabilitation focus is absent. Assessment coverage differs across governorates, so the ranking should be used to guide further review rather than as a final funding decision.
